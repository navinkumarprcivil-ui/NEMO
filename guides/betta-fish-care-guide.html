Betta Fish Care Guide

Nemo Aqua Store · Updated July 2026

Bettas are among the most rewarding fish for beginners — hardy, interactive and stunning. But bowls and jars shorten their lives. Here is how to keep a betta thriving for 3–5 years.

Tank size & setup

  • Minimum 10 litres, 20 litres is better. Wider is better than taller.
  • Gentle filter (bettas dislike strong current) and a lid — they jump.
  • Heater set to 26–28°C. Bettas are tropical; cold water causes lethargy and fin rot.
  • Soft silk or live plants to rest on. Avoid sharp plastic decor that tears fins.

Feeding

  • Quality betta pellets, 2–4 pellets twice a day.
  • Treats 1–2× a week: frozen bloodworms or brine shrimp.
  • One fasting day per week prevents bloating.

Water care

  • Change 25–30% of the water weekly. Always use a water conditioner.
  • Cycle the tank before adding the fish — see our aquarium cycling guide.

Tank mates

Male bettas must be kept solo or with peaceful bottom dwellers (corydoras, kuhli loaches, snails). Never two males together, and avoid fin-nippers like tiger barbs or colourful guppies that trigger aggression.

Common mistake: keeping a betta in an unheated bowl. In most of India winter nights drop below 20°C — a small 25W heater is the single best upgrade for your betta's health.

Frequently asked questions

How long do betta fish live?

With a heated, filtered 10–20 litre tank and good food, bettas live 3–5 years. In bowls, often less than a year.

Can betta fish live with other fish?

Males must not be kept with other bettas. Peaceful bottom dwellers like corydoras and snails are usually fine in 40+ litre tanks.

What temperature do bettas need?

26–28°C. Below 24°C bettas become lethargic and prone to disease, so a heater is recommended in most Indian homes outside summer.

How often should I feed my betta?

2–4 quality pellets twice daily, with one fasting day per week. Overfeeding is the most common cause of illness.
