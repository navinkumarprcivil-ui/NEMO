10 Best Aquarium Fish for Beginners

Nemo Aqua Store · Updated July 2026

Start with hardy, peaceful species that forgive beginner mistakes. All ten below thrive in Indian climates and are easy to find.

The list

  1. Guppy — colourful, active, breeds readily. 20L+, keep 1 male : 2 females.
  2. Betta — stunning and interactive. 10–20L, males solo. See our betta care guide.
  3. Zebra Danio — nearly indestructible, loves to school. 40L+, keep 6+.
  4. Neon Tetra — the classic glowing school. 40L+, keep 8+, stable cycled water.
  5. Platy — peaceful, hardy livebearer in many colours. 40L+.
  6. Corydoras catfish — adorable bottom cleaners. 40L+, keep 4+, soft sand preferred.
  7. Molly — hardy and friendly. 60L+, slightly hard water is ideal.
  8. Cherry Barb — peaceful barb (unlike tiger barbs!). 40L+, keep 6+.
  9. Dwarf Gourami — a centrepiece fish with personality. 40L+, one male per tank.
  10. White Cloud Minnow — thrives even without a heater. 20L+, keep 6+.
Golden rule: cycle the tank first (how to cycle), stock slowly, and never mix fin-nippers with long-finned fish.

Fish to avoid as a beginner

  • Goldfish in bowls — they need 75L+ and strong filtration.
  • Angelfish/Oscars — grow large and territorial.
  • “Shark” species — most outgrow home tanks.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest fish to keep alive?

Zebra danios and guppies are widely considered the hardiest beginner fish — tolerant of temperature swings and beginner mistakes.

How many fish can I keep in a 40 litre tank?

A rough starting rule is 1 cm of adult fish per litre after cycling — e.g. a school of 8 neon tetras plus a few corydoras.

Do beginner fish need a heater?

Most tropical species prefer 24–28°C. White cloud minnows are a good no-heater choice for cooler homes.

Which fish should NOT be kept together?

Avoid mixing fin-nippers (tiger barbs, some tetras) with long-finned fish like bettas and guppies, and never keep two male bettas together.
