Best Aquarium Plants for Beginners

Nemo Aqua Store · Updated July 2026

Live plants absorb fish waste, fight algae, and make fish feel at home — and these seven grow happily with basic lights and no CO2 injection.

The easy seven

  1. Java Fern — tie to wood or rock (don't bury the rhizome). Nearly unkillable.
  2. Anubias — thick glossy leaves, thrives in shade. Also attaches to hardscape.
  3. Amazon Sword — big background plant; push a root tab into the substrate.
  4. Vallisneria — grass-like runners that quickly fill the back wall.
  5. Water Wisteria — fast grower that soaks up excess nutrients (algae's enemy).
  6. Cryptocoryne wendtii — low, bushy midground plant; may melt on arrival, then regrows stronger.
  7. Marimo Moss Ball — zero effort; just roll it over occasionally.

Planting tips

  • Rhizome plants (java fern, anubias) must sit on wood/rock — burying the rhizome rots them.
  • Rooted plants (swords, vallisneria, crypts) want 4–5 cm of substrate and a root tab every few months.
  • 6–8 hours of light daily on a timer. More light without CO2 = algae.
  • Some melt after transport is normal — trim dead leaves and wait; new growth adapts to your water.
Bonus: plants carry beneficial bacteria that speed up cycling a new tank — plant heavily from day one.

Frequently asked questions

Which aquarium plant is easiest to keep alive?

Java fern and anubias — both grow in low light, need no CO2, and attach to wood or rock instead of substrate.

Do aquarium plants need CO2?

Not the beginner species in this list. CO2 speeds growth but java fern, anubias, crypts and vallisneria grow fine without it.

Why are my new plants melting?

Melting after transport is normal, especially for crypts. Trim dying leaves; the plant regrows leaves adapted to your water within weeks.

Do live plants reduce algae?

Yes — fast growers like water wisteria outcompete algae for nutrients. Combined with a light timer, they are the best natural algae control.
