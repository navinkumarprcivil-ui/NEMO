Best Aquarium Filters Under ₹1000

Nemo Aqua Store · Updated July 2026

You don't need an expensive canister to keep water crystal clear. Under ₹1000, three filter types cover almost every tank up to 100 litres — here's how to choose.

1. Sponge filter (₹150–₹400) — best for breeders & small tanks

  • Runs on an air pump; gentle flow that won't stress bettas, fry or shrimp.
  • Huge surface for beneficial bacteria — great biological filtration.
  • Downside: needs a separate air pump (~₹300) and looks utilitarian.

2. Internal power filter (₹300–₹700) — best all-rounder for 20–60L

  • Submerged unit with a pump and sponge cartridge; simple and silent.
  • Choose one rated at 4–5× your tank volume per hour (e.g. 200 L/h for a 40L tank).
  • Downside: takes up space inside the tank; rinse the sponge weekly.

3. Hang-on-back / HOB filter (₹500–₹1000) — best for 40–100L display tanks

  • Hangs outside, so no space lost inside; easy to clean and to add media.
  • Waterfall return adds oxygen. Our 300 L/h HOB filter handles tanks up to 60–80L.
  • Downside: slight hum if the tank level drops low.

Sizing rule of thumb

Flow rate ≥ 4× tank volume per hour, and never wash filter media in tap water — rinse in removed tank water so you don't kill the bacteria colony (see why cycling matters).

Our pick: under ₹500 → sponge filter + air pump for bettas/shrimp; ₹500–₹1000 → a HOB for community tanks. Skip “filter + light combo” hoods — both parts are usually weak.

Frequently asked questions

Which filter is best for a small betta tank?

A sponge filter or a low-flow internal filter. Bettas struggle in strong current, so gentle flow matters more than raw power.

How do I size an aquarium filter?

Pick a flow rate at least 4–5× your tank volume per hour — a 40 litre tank needs roughly a 200 L/h filter.

How often should I clean the filter?

Rinse the sponge every 1–2 weeks in water removed during a water change — never under tap water, which kills beneficial bacteria.

Can a filter be too strong?

Yes. Long-finned fish like bettas and fry get exhausted in strong current. If fish struggle to swim, baffle the outflow or choose a gentler filter.
