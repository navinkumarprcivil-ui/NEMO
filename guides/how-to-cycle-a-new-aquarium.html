How to Cycle a New Aquarium

Nemo Aqua Store · Updated July 2026

The #1 reason new aquarium fish die is “new tank syndrome” — adding fish to an uncycled tank. Cycling grows the invisible bacteria that convert toxic fish waste (ammonia) into harmless nitrate.

The nitrogen cycle in 30 seconds

  1. Fish waste and uneaten food release ammonia (highly toxic).
  2. Bacteria colony #1 converts ammonia → nitrite (still toxic).
  3. Bacteria colony #2 converts nitrite → nitrate (safe at low levels, removed by water changes and plants).

Growing both colonies takes 3–5 weeks. Until then, ammonia and nitrite spike and poison fish.

Step-by-step fishless cycle

  1. Set up tank, filter, heater. Fill with conditioned water and run 24×7.
  2. Add an ammonia source: a pinch of fish food every 2 days, or bottled ammonia.
  3. From week 2, test the water every few days with a liquid test kit.
  4. You'll see ammonia spike → fall, then nitrite spike → fall.
  5. When ammonia and nitrite both read 0 and nitrate is rising — you're cycled.
  6. Do a 50% water change, then add fish a few at a time.
Shortcut: a handful of gravel or a used filter sponge from an established, disease-free tank can cut cycling time in half. Live plants also absorb ammonia and speed things up.

After cycling

  • Add fish gradually — the bacteria colony grows with the load.
  • Weekly 25–30% water changes keep nitrate low.
  • Never wash filter media in tap water; rinse it in tank water to protect the bacteria.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to cycle an aquarium?

Typically 3–5 weeks for a fishless cycle. Seeding with media from an established tank can reduce this to 1–2 weeks.

Can I cycle a tank with fish in it?

It is possible but stressful for the fish and needs near-daily water changes. A fishless cycle is safer and easier for beginners.

How do I know my tank is cycled?

A water test shows 0 ammonia, 0 nitrite and some nitrate, even a day after adding an ammonia source.

Do live plants help cycling?

Yes — plants consume ammonia directly and carry helpful bacteria on their leaves, making the cycle faster and more stable.
